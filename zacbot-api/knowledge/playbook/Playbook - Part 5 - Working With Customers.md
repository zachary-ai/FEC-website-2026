# Part 5: Working With Customers (ZacBot Edition)

## Engagement Models

**Workshops/Discovery:** 2-4 hours, $2-5K. Low-risk, high-value. Great diagnostic tool for testing fit.

**Consulting Projects:** Fixed scope, 4-12 weeks, $10-30K. Clear deliverables. Risk: scope creep. Get boundaries in writing.

**Coaching/Advisory:** Regular 1:1s, monthly or bi-weekly, $1-3K/month. Sounding board for founders who don't need you in the weeds.

**Fractional Retainer:** 1-2 days/week, $5-15K/month, 3-6 month minimum. Deep integration with the business. You're an operator, not a consultant. Key difference: a consultant tells you what to do, a fractional does it with you.

**Trust-building path:** Workshop ($2-5K) -> Project ($10-30K) -> Retainer ($5-15K/month). Everyone tests fit with less risk.

## The First 30 Days

### Days 1-5: Deep Dive (Diagnostic)
- Talk to founder/CEO, leadership team, people doing actual work, customers
- Pull the data: CRM, financials, pipeline, customer feedback. What people tell you vs what data shows are often completely different.
- Map stakeholders, political landmines, what's been tried before

### Days 6-10: Quick Wins
Identify 2-3 high-visibility, low-effort improvements. Not transformational, just proof you know what you're doing.
- Fix an obvious gap in a process
- Set up a dashboard giving founder visibility they didn't have
- Introduce a framework for a meeting that's currently wasted time
Quick wins buy time and credibility for bigger work.

### Days 11-20: Present Findings
Simple, clear assessment:
- Here's what I found
- Here's what I recommend
- Here's the priority order
- Here's what success looks like in 90 days
One document. Clear language. Specific recommendations with timelines and owners.

### Days 21-30: Build Momentum
- Start building systems and processes
- Get team aligned on priorities
- Establish your rhythm
- Start tracking against the 90-day plan

## The 90-Day Sprint

Every engagement should have a 90-day plan with:
- Clear objectives (what does done look like?)
- Key deliverables with dates
- Prioritized actions in order
- Weekly milestones with specific owners
- Agreed success metrics

**Why 90 days:** Long enough for real change, short enough for urgency, aligns with quarterly cycles, forces focus.

Review at 90 days: what worked, what didn't, what's next. Natural checkpoint for both parties.

## Communication System

### Weekly Executive Check-ins (Non-Negotiable)
30-minute weekly call with your sponsor:
- Progress against plan
- Flag blockers, get decisions
- Align priorities for coming week
- Surface risks early

Skip this and the founder starts making assumptions.

### Visual Progress Tracking
Keep a shared document (Notion, Google Docs, Figma). Update weekly. Make progress visible. If value isn't visible to the person paying invoices, there will be questions.

### Stakeholder Management
Map everyone with a stake: who needs to be informed, consulted, who can block you, who are your allies. Manage proactively with quick messages, coffee, shout-outs.

## Managing Multiple Clients

### Context Switching
- Dedicate specific days to specific clients
- Keep detailed notes
- Use separate workspaces/browser profiles per client
- Build 15-minute buffer between client work
- Start each client day reviewing last session's notes

### Time Boundaries
If you're engaged for one day a week, that's one day. Not one day plus "just a quick call" plus "just checking Slack" on Friday. Scope creep on time is the fastest way to burn out and devalue your offering. Need more? That's a conversation about expanding the engagement.

### The Capacity Trap
Better to have 2 clients at $10K than 4 at $5K. Every additional client adds cognitive load, communication overhead, and scheduling complexity.

## When It's Time to Leave

Plan your exit from day one. You're here to build capability, not create dependency.

**Signs to transition out:**
- Systems running without you
- Team executes the playbook independently
- You've hired/developed someone to take over
- Founder no longer relies on you for day-to-day decisions

**How to exit well:**
- Give 4-6 weeks notice
- Document everything you've built
- Train the successor
- Offer lighter advisory arrangement for transition
- Leave the door open

Best compliment: "We don't need you anymore."

## Non-Negotiables

1. Proper discovery upfront (align on current state, outcomes, timeline, metrics, roadblocks)
2. Regular review sessions, weekly ideal, fortnightly minimum

## Pro Tips

- Always have a written scope
- Define success early, agree on it, write it down
- Be clear about what's NOT included
- Build in flexibility for scope changes (with a process)
- Document everything
- Have an exit clause for both parties
- Embedded but not an employee, keep boundaries clear
