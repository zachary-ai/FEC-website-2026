# Part 2: Positioning Yourself (ZacBot Edition)

## Defining Your Value Proposition

Your value prop is the specific reason companies choose to work with you. Not your life story, not career highlights.

**How to figure it out:**
- What problems can you actually solve? (not theoretically)
- What unique perspective do you bring?
- What results have you delivered? (real numbers, real impact)
- What's your superpower? (the thing colleagues always came to you for)

**Common traps:**
- Making it about you ("I have 20 years experience..." nobody cares)
- Being too vague ("I help companies grow", how exactly?)
- Copying others
- Making it too complex (if you can't explain it to your mum, simplify)

**Good value props:**
- "I help B2B SaaS companies fix their broken sales processes in 90 days or less"
- "I take early-stage startups from idea to revenue-generating in 6 months"
- "I help funded startups stop burning cash on marketing that doesn't work"

**Bad value props:**
- "I'm a seasoned executive with deep experience in driving growth"
- "I help companies reach their full potential"
- "Strategic leader driving transformation"

**Your value prop should be:**
- Specific enough that some people say "that's not for me"
- Focused on outcomes, not process
- Something you can actually deliver on

**Frameworks:** Use the Value Proposition Canvas (aligns features/benefits with customer needs) or Steve Blank template: "We help (X) do (Y) by doing (Z)."

**Test it:** Say it out loud. If people squint or say "oh... interesting..." go back to the drawing board. When they say "I know someone who needs exactly that!" you're onto something.

## Defining Your Niche

The narrower your focus, the easier it is to get clients. If your niche feels uncomfortably narrow, you might be in the right place.

**Why niche down:**
- Easier to spot ideal clients
- Higher rates (specialists command premiums)
- Faster trust-building
- More referrals (people remember specific things)
- Less competition

**Finding your niche questions:**
- What industries have you worked in?
- Where have you had the most success?
- What problems do you genuinely enjoy solving?
- Where do you have provable results?

**Good niches:**
- "I help bootstrapped SaaS companies fix their customer churn"
- "I build sales engines for B2B marketplaces"
- "I help funded D2C brands scale their manufacturing"

**Bad niches:**
- "I help startups with strategy" (too vague)
- "I do fractional CXO work" (not a niche)
- "I help any business that needs me" (not focused)

**Niche validation checklist:**
1. Can you name 20 companies that fit?
2. Do you have case studies in this space?
3. Can you speak their language fluently?
4. Are there active communities in this area?
5. Is there evidence they spend money on this problem?

**Don't worry about limiting yourself.** Your work naturally expands once you're in. Clients ask you to do more. You can have multiple niches, just don't start there. Being known for one thing beats being unknown for many things.

## Determining Your ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)

**Ideal customers:**
- Have the problem you solve best
- Value your specific experience
- Can afford your services
- Make decisions relatively quickly
- Respect professional boundaries
- Actually implement your advice

**Build your ICP around:**

Company demographics: size (revenue, employees), industry, stage (startup/scale-up/mature), geography, tech stack.

Decision maker profile: role/title, budget authority, pain points, goals, where they hang out online/IRL.

**Red flags (Anti-ICP):**
- Want everything done yesterday
- Expect miracles on a shoestring
- "Just need someone to execute"
- Have burned through multiple consultants
- Can't clearly articulate their goals

**Your ICP shapes:** Where you network, what content you create, how you price, which opportunities you pursue, the language you use.

**ICP validation:**
1. Share it with your network
2. Find 10 companies that match perfectly
3. Find their decision makers on LinkedIn
4. Check if they're discussing your focus area
5. Verify they're spending money on the problem

Adjust your ICP based on real experience: which clients you enjoy, who gets best results, where natural referrals come from.

## Service Offerings

**Workshop/Discovery Sessions:** 2-4 hours, $2-5K. Great for establishing trust, often leads to longer engagements.

**Consulting Projects:** Fixed scope, 4-12 weeks, $10-30K. Good for testing working relationship.

**Coaching:** Regular 1:1 sessions, monthly or bi-weekly, $1-3K/month. Less hands-on.

**Fractional (Monthly Retainer):** 1-2 days/week, $5-15K/month, 6-12 month minimum. Deep integration, focused on impact not hours.

**Trust-building path:** Workshop -> Small project -> Ongoing fractional role. Lower risk for both parties.

**Non-negotiables:**
1. Proper discovery/workshop upfront (align on current state, outcomes, timeline, success metrics, roadblocks)
2. Regular review sessions, weekly ideal, fortnightly at most (progress check, roadblock removal, priority adjustment)

**Pro tips:**
- Always have a written scope
- Define success early
- Be clear about what's NOT included
- Build in flexibility for scope changes
- Document everything
- Have an exit clause for both parties
- Keep progress visible to the CEO/founder paying invoices

## Setting Your Rates

**Salary Breakdown Method:** Last salary + 40% (benefits/super) / number of clients = per client annual rate. Example: $250K + 40% = $350K / 3 clients = ~$10K/month per client.

**Lifestyle Math:** Monthly needs + business expenses / target clients. Example: $15K/month + $5K expenses = $7K minimum per client with 3 clients.

**Market benchmarks (AU):**
- Early-stage startups: $5-8K/month
- Growth stage: $8-12K/month
- Enterprise: $12-15K+/month

**Key principles:**
- Better to have 2 clients at $10K than 4 at $5K (context switching is real)
- Be ready to walk away if numbers don't work
- You're selling the founder's time savings and your operating bandwidth, not hours

## What Problems You Solve

Be specific. Pattern match from your career wins.

**Problem categories:** Technical leadership, sales & revenue, product & strategy, people & talent, transformation.

**Examples:**
- Building outbound enterprise sales for a B2B2C marketplace
- Creating reseller programs that generated pipeline deals in 60 days
- Repositioning companies (pushing margins from zero to 60%)
- Building new market presence (10:1 ROI entering US markets)
- Upskilling 100+ strategists on ABM programs

**Red flags (problems you can't solve):**
- Problems you've only seen from a distance
- Problems needing deep institutional knowledge
- Problems requiring large teams to execute
- Problems needing years to solve
- Problems outside your core expertise

## What You Actually Bring

**Acceleration & de-risking:** Pattern recognition, pre-built playbooks, tested vendor relationships, common pitfall avoidance.

**Strategic + tactical combination:** Hands-on execution (not just PowerPoints). This differentiates fractionals from consultants. Board management, reality checks, founder sounding board.

**Network effects:** Access to specialists, investors, peer companies, talent, industry insights.

**Knowledge transfer:** Upskill teams while doing the work. Build repeatable processes. Create business-specific playbooks. Leave the business stronger.

**The X Factor:** Speed to impact, no corporate politics, flexible scaling, blend of outside perspective and deep involvement.

**Don't offer:**
- Being everything to everyone
- Promising overnight miracles
- Taking over permanently
- Being available 24/7
