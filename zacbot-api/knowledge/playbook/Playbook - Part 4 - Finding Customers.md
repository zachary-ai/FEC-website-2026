# Part 4: Finding Customers (ZacBot Edition)

## First Steps

1. Lock in your niche and value proposition
2. Set up a simple website (Carrd or Webflow, nothing fancy)
3. Update LinkedIn profile for fractional focus
4. Make a LinkedIn announcement
5. Reach out to your network for coffee catchups

Your first client is likely in your network. Frame initial meetings as feedback sessions, not sales calls: "I'm moving into fractional work focused on [niche]. Would love your thoughts."

Network with other fractionals. This is the #1 lead source for many people after your own network.

## Lead Sources (Ranked)

1. Referrals from happy clients
2. Your existing network
3. Content and thought leadership
4. Direct outreach
5. Everything else

## Volume Context

Fractional is a low-volume game. If you take 3 one-day-per-week clients with 6-month average engagements, you need just 6 new clients per year. That's under 2 per quarter.

## Building Your Referral Engine

**Key reframe:** Don't ask for "referrals." Ask for "introductions to people you might be able to help."

**Scripts that work:**

After unsuccessful discovery call: "It seems like we're not the right fit, but you have a good handle on what I offer. Do you know anyone I should be talking to?"

To happy client: "Really glad we could help with [specific win]. I'm looking to connect with other [role] at [company type] facing similar challenges. Anyone come to mind?"

Broader network: "I'm focusing on helping [type] companies solve [problem]. Would love your thoughts and to see if anyone comes to mind."

**Make it easy:**
- Be specific about who you're looking for
- Write the intro email for them
- Follow up (don't be annoying)

**Best times to ask:**
- Right after delivering a win
- When a client gives unprompted praise
- During positive review sessions
- When wrapping up a successful project
- After a "no" (some of your best intros come from prospects who aren't ready)

**Systematize it:**
- Build into engagement wrap-up process
- Set quarterly network nurture reminders
- Keep a "happy customer" folder for testimonials
- Track where introductions come from and reciprocate

## Content Strategy

LinkedIn is king for fractionals. Your content establishes authority and serves as your CV (no one asks for a traditional CV).

**Content tactics:**
1. Strong personal brand across LinkedIn
2. Thought leadership content (articles, posts, white papers)
3. Case studies showcasing results
4. Webinars or virtual workshops
5. Guest posts in industry publications
6. Video content (LinkedIn prioritizes video)

**Posting cadence:**
- 3x weekly LinkedIn posts
- Monthly blog articles
- Quarterly whitepapers or ebooks
- Guest posts on industry sites
- Speaking at events or podcasts

Use AI (ChatGPT, Claude) to speed up content creation while maintaining quality.

## Outbound

**Tools:**
- LinkedIn Sales Navigator ($99/month) for prospect research
- Apollo.io for email finding and campaigns (free tier available)
- PhantomBuster for LinkedIn automation
- Loom for personalized video messages

**Multi-channel approach:**
1. LinkedIn connection + personalized note
2. Follow up with value-add email
3. Engage with their content before pitching
4. Share relevant case studies

**Content-led outreach:**
- Share your content first
- Comment on prospect's posts
- Offer genuine insights before selling
- Position yourself as a peer, not a vendor

**Key success factors:**
- Research prospects thoroughly before reaching out
- Personalize every message (use SMYKM framework)
- Focus on their specific challenges
- Provide value before asking for anything
- Follow up consistently but not aggressively
- Quality over quantity: 10 personalized attempts beat 100 generic messages

**Lead prioritization:**
- Target companies matching your ICP
- Look for trigger events (funding, leadership changes, growth signals)
- Focus on decision-makers with budget authority
- Prioritize warm over cold

**Measuring success:** Track connection acceptance rates, response rates, meeting conversion rates, which approaches work best.

## Networking

- Be strategic about which events you attend
- Offer value before asking for anything
- Follow up and stay top of mind
- Proactively make connections and introductions
- Two-way street: always help others

## The Flywheel

Content demonstrates expertise -> fuels outbound -> makes networking effective -> success creates case studies and referrals -> feeds back into content -> each component reinforces the others, building momentum over time.
