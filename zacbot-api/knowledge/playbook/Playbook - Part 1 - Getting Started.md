# Part 1: Getting Started (ZacBot Edition)

## Who This Is For

**Good fit:**
- 10+ years executive experience
- Comfortable with ambiguity, self-motivated
- Financially stable enough to handle irregular income
- Ready to run your own business (this IS entrepreneurship)

**Not a good fit:**
- Need predictable 9-5 with steady paychecks
- Struggle with self-promotion, networking, or saying no
- Early career, still building core skills
- Think it will be easier than full-time (it's often more work)

**You'll wear these hats:** Business developer, service provider, account manager, operations manager, finance department.

**Traits of successful fractionals:** Deep expertise, strong communication, comfort with ambiguity, natural problem-solving, high EQ, pragmatic approach.

## Common Motivations

**Sustainable drivers:**
- Impact without corporate politics
- See results in months, not years
- Help multiple companies simultaneously
- Learning across industries and contexts
- Control your schedule, pick your clients, say no to toxic situations

**Motivations that won't sustain you:**
- Just want more money
- Think it'll be easier than full-time
- Burned out and need a break
- Can't find a full-time role
- Want to work less

Write down your real "why." You'll need it when a client is difficult, pipeline is dry, or a cushy full-time offer lands.

## Making the Decision

**Before you quit your day job:**
- Have 6 months of expenses saved
- Know your absolute minimum monthly income
- Have a plan for super/retirement contributions
- Talk to your partner first. This affects the whole household.

**Test the waters first:**
- Start with a side project (if your contract allows)
- Take on one small client while still employed
- Do pro-bono work to test your systems
- Build your content and personal brand before you need it

**Support system you'll need:**
- Good accountant
- Basic legal template for contracts
- Mentor/peer who's done this before
- Workspace that works for you
- Basic tech stack

## Decision Framework

Ask yourself honestly:
1. Can I handle irregular income?
2. Am I ready to sell myself and my services?
3. Do I have enough experience to provide real value?
4. Can I work without traditional job structure?
5. Can I handle multiple clients and contexts simultaneously?

"No" to any doesn't mean don't do it. It means address that thing first.

## The Mindset Shift

You're not changing jobs. You're becoming a business owner, taking full responsibility for your income, building something from scratch.

You can always go back to full-time if it doesn't work out. Most successful fractionals just picked a date, lined up one client, and jumped.
